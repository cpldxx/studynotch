Lecture 9, Differentiability of functions of two variables

9.2 Differentiability

Definition. We say f is differentiable at (a,b) if the error of its linear approximation L goes to zero faster than the distance to (a,b):

|f(x,y) − L(x,y)| / ‖(x,y) − (a,b)‖ → 0  as  (x,y) → (a,b).

Theorem 2. If f is differentiable at (a,b), then f is continuous at (a,b).

Remark. The converse is false: a function can have both partial derivatives at a point and still fail to be continuous there. The next example shows how this happens along a diagonal.

Example 3. Let f(x,y) = xy / (x² + y²) for (x,y) ≠ (0,0) and f(0,0) = 0. Both partial derivatives exist at the origin, since f vanishes on the two axes.

One study hour, seven scenes

This is a demo screen. The numbers and answers are prepared examples; in the app, Claude writes the answers.

  1. Press the notch and the island opens, with the mascot settling into the card. Pick a course and press “Start”. Click anywhere else, or leave it alone for 15 seconds, and it folds back.

  2. During a session, only the mascot and the minutes left stay beside the notch. No seconds: a number that keeps changing is the first thing your eye catches. Point at it for a one-line summary under the island.

  3. Select some text and right-click: “Ask” is at the top of the menu. No digging through the Services submenu. It’s there outside a session too.

  4. A small window opens where you clicked. Pick a button such as “I don’t get this”, or type your own question, then press “Send”. Take a screenshot during a session and the same window opens with the picture.

  5. It finds which page of your course PDFs the sentence is on and explains with that page in view. No Claude window appears. Under the answer, you can narrow down what is still unclear.

  6. It doesn’t block sites. Stay more than a minute somewhere on your distraction list and a card appears; if you don’t react, it folds after 8 seconds. It never shows while you’re typing.

  7. Your focus time, drift time and questions are recorded. Next time you start the same course, what you got stuck on comes first.

Press an underlined sentence or the black notch on the screen, or step through the numbers in order. The app works the same way: everything is a click, with no shortcuts to remember.

It never blocks you. It shows you, with a face.

A small glossy drop lives at one end of the island and shows how things are going with its eyes, which also follow your pointer. Pick an expression. Press it and it hops; drag it and it spins.

Outside a session it just watches your pointer and blinks now and then.

The Claude you already use answers in the background

StudyNotch has no server. The first thing it asks is which AI should answer: Claude, ChatGPT or Gemini, each with your own account. Only the one you connect gets your questions. It runs the Claude Code that ships inside the app in the background, takes only the answer, and never opens a Claude window.

How to connect

It never clicks the Claude app’s window or scrapes its screen. It only runs the official tool, signed in to the same account.

An API key is stored only in your Mac’s Keychain. With no server, there is nowhere else for it to go.

What you put in a course folder is what it knows

Each course has a folder. Put your lecture PDFs and textbook in it, and when you pick that course for a session, the app answers with that material in mind.

~/StudyNotch/Courses/MATH 237/
├── course.md    textbook, progress, your professor’s notation
├── materials/   lecture PDFs, textbook
├── cards.md     review cards
└── log/         questions and answers by date

Link a folder you already keep, or drag PDFs onto the notch. During a session they join that course.

If the answer isn’t in your material, it says “not from your material” at the end. It never pretends otherwise.

Only what goes into a question leaves your Mac

Distraction checks happen entirely on this Mac. What you looked at is never sent anywhere.

Stays on your Mac

  • Which apps and sites you looked at
  • Session history, focus and drift time
  • Every PDF in your course folders
  • Review cards and past questions

Sent to the AI when you ask

  • The selected text, or one screenshot
  • Your course notes and the text of the matching page
  • Any page of the course’s PDFs that the AI opens to answer
  • The name and window title of the app you were reading in
  • Your question

No usage statistics are collected.

To draw math in answers, a math library is loaded from the internet. The next version will ship it inside the app.

Questions

Does it cost anything?

Not right now: this is an early version and the app is free. Answers come out of your existing Claude subscription. If you connect an API key, you pay for what you use.

Does it block distracting sites?

No, it only tells you. Press “It’s for study” and that place won’t count as a distraction for the rest of the session.

Which apps can I read in?

It is built around Preview, Safari and Chrome-based browsers. Apps hand over selected text in different ways, so if your text can’t be read, take a screenshot during a session and ask about that instead.

Does it work offline?

The timer and drift cards do. To get answers, it needs to reach the AI you connected.

What about a Mac without a notch, or an external display?

It appears as a small black bar in the middle of the menu bar. You choose which screen it lives on from the menu bar icon.

Is the app in English?

Yes. Answers come back in the language you type: ask in Korean and the answer is in Korean. A button pressed on its own gets an English answer.

Does it work on iPhone or iPad?

Not yet. It is Mac only for now.

Build it from source and use it today

There is no download yet. One command builds it, and on first launch the notch walks you through connecting an AI and granting permissions, one step at a time.
